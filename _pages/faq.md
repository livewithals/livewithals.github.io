<div class="faq-item">
<div class="faq-q">When do PALS need a BiPAP?</div>
As soon as shallow rapid breathing is noticed, especially when lying flat.
</div>

<div class="faq-item">
<div class="faq-q">When do PALS need a feeding tube?</div>
As long as PALS can swallow without coughing it is fine. But in case of coughing while eating, swallowing difficulty, or slow chewing — a feeding tube should be placed, no matter what the doctor says.
</div>

<div class="faq-item">
<div class="faq-q">When do PALS need a Tracheostomy?</div>

1. When PALS lose the ability to spit out or swallow secretions, leading to chest congestion, saliva seeping into the lungs, visible respiratory distress, low saturation, frequent pneumonia, and poor quality of life.
2. When PALS become dependent on BiPAP 24×7 and are unable to tolerate the mask full-time.

</div>

<div class="faq-item">
<div class="faq-q">BiPAP or Oxygen or Both?</div>

1. PALS with diaphragm weakness need **BiPAP**
2. PALS with pneumonia (no diaphragm weakness) need **Oxygen**
3. PALS with both conditions need **BiPAP + Oxygen**
4. In an emergency when unsure — use both to be safe
5. Periodic re-evaluation of BiPAP settings is essential as progression continues

</div>

<div class="faq-item">
<div class="faq-q">Does Tracheostomy mean being dependent on a ventilator full-time?</div>
No.
</div>

<div class="faq-item">
<div class="faq-q">Determining whether to pursue a tracheostomy for an ALS patient</div>

Deciding whether to proceed with a tracheostomy involves careful consideration with healthcare professionals, caregivers, and the patient. Key factors:

1. **Quality of Life** — Assess current quality of life and preferences. Discuss how tracheostomy might impact communication, eating, and interaction.
2. **Disease Progression** — Understand the stage of ALS. Tracheostomy is often considered when respiratory function declines significantly.
3. **Medical Evaluation** — Consult neurologists and pulmonologists to evaluate respiratory status, including lung function tests.
4. **Family and Caregiver Support** — Tracheostomy requires ongoing care including suctioning, tube care, and monitoring for complications.
5. **Patient Wishes** — Respect the patient's autonomy and involve them in decisions as much as possible.
6. **Risks and Benefits** — Understand potential complications such as infection, discomfort, and quality of life impact.
7. **Advance Directives** — Review any living wills outlining preferences for end-of-life care.

The decision should be made collaboratively with open and honest discussions with healthcare professionals and loved ones.

</div>
